# HR Analytics Dashboard – Employee Attrition Analysis

## 📌 Project Overview

The **HR Analytics Dashboard** is an interactive Power BI project designed to analyze employee data and understand the key factors influencing employee attrition. It provides insights into employee demographics, salary, job roles, departments, experience, and business travel patterns to support data-driven HR decision-making.

The dashboard helps HR professionals identify attrition trends, compare employee turnover across different categories, and explore potential factors associated with employees leaving the organization.

## 🎯 Project Objectives

- Analyze employee data, including salary, department, age, and experience.
- Identify patterns and trends in employee attrition.
- Perform correlation analysis to explore relationships between employee attributes and attrition.
- Compare attrition across departments, job roles, education fields, and salary slabs.
- Calculate key HR performance indicators such as attrition rate, retention rate, average age, and average tenure.
- Build an interactive Power BI dashboard to support HR analytics and decision-making.

## 🛠️ Tools & Technologies

- **Power BI** – Interactive dashboard development and data visualization
- **Power Query** – Data cleaning and transformation
- **DAX (Data Analysis Expressions)** – KPI calculations and measures
- **Microsoft Excel / CSV** – Dataset storage and preparation
- **Data Analytics** – Exploratory analysis and identification of attrition patterns

## 📈 Dashboard Features

### 1. Employee Attrition Analysis
- Analyzes the number of employees who left the organization.
- Calculates the overall attrition rate.
- Displays employee retention and workforce statistics.

### 2. Attrition by Education
- Examines attrition across different educational backgrounds and fields.
- Helps identify differences in attrition distribution among employee groups.

### 3. Attrition by Age Group
- Compares attrition counts across age groups.
- Visualizes attrition trends and rates across different age categories.

### 4. Attrition by Salary Slab
- Categorizes employees into salary ranges.
- Compares attrition counts across different salary slabs.

### 5. Attrition by Years at Company
- Analyzes attrition across employee tenure.
- Helps identify tenure ranges where employee exits are more frequent.

### 6. Job Role and Job Level Analysis
- Compares attrition counts across job roles and job levels.
- Helps HR teams understand how employee exits are distributed across positions.

### 7. Attrition by Department
- Compares attrition across departments, including Research & Development, Sales, and Human Resources.
- Supports department-level workforce planning.

### 8. Attrition by Marital Status
- Analyzes attrition distribution across marital-status categories.
- Provides additional demographic insights.

### 9. Attrition by Business Travel
- Examines attrition patterns associated with business travel categories.
- Helps investigate whether travel requirements are associated with employee turnover.

### 10. Interactive Filters
The dashboard includes slicers for:
- Age Group
- Business Travel
- Department
- Education Field

These filters allow users to explore employee attrition from different perspectives.

## 🔍 Key Insights

Based on the dashboard:

- **Overall attrition:** 238 out of 1,480 employees left the organization, resulting in an attrition rate of 16.08%.
- **Employee retention:** The workforce retention rate is approximately 83.92%.
- **Department-level patterns:** Research & Development accounts for the highest attrition count in the displayed department comparison, followed by Sales and Human Resources.
- **Education distribution:** Attrition is distributed across multiple education fields, with Life Sciences representing the largest share in the displayed chart.
- **Marital status:** Single employees account for approximately 50.42% of the attrition distribution shown.
- **Workforce profile:** The average employee age is 36.92 years, and average tenure is 7.01 years.

These observations describe patterns in the displayed data and do not, by themselves, establish the causes of employee attrition.
